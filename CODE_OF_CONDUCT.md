# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people and be decent to them. Those are not in tension.

## What is expected

This project is about being right, and being right requires people to say when
something is wrong. Direct technical disagreement is welcome and is not rudeness.
Reviewing a change hard, pointing out that a measurement does not support its
conclusion, or saying plainly that an approach will not work are all normal and
useful.

What is expected alongside that:

- **Criticise the work, not the person.** "This measurement conflates latency
  with throughput" is useful. "You clearly do not understand GPUs" is not, and
  is also usually wrong.
- **Bring evidence.** A claim about the hardware should come with the command
  that demonstrates it. This applies to everyone, including the maintainer.
- **Accept correction the same way you give it.** Being wrong about an encoding
  is the normal condition of this work. Several documented findings here began
  as the maintainer's own mistakes.
- **Assume the other person is trying.** Reverse engineering is confusing and
  most confusion is honest.

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning comments, unwelcome attention, and
publishing anyone's private information. None of that has anything to do with
being technically direct, and being technically direct is not a defence for it.

## Scope

Applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, and anywhere someone is
representing the project.

## Reporting

Email sunnypatel124555@gmail.com. Reports are read by the maintainer and are not
shared further without your agreement, except where it is necessary to act on
them.

The maintainer will respond, and may edit or remove contributions, or block an
account, when this document is not being followed. Where something is a
judgement call, the reasoning will be stated rather than the decision simply
being made.
